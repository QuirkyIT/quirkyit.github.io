02 / Prioritise the threat

Turn exposure into decisions your business can defend.

Understand the actors targeting your industry, what they want and how they operate. We connect that intelligence to your exposed systems and business processes, so your board and cyber team can see which risks deserve attention and why.

Your exposure report establishes what is visible. The threat briefing adds sector intelligence, actor motivation and documented tradecraft: the techniques attackers use. Each risk judgement connects that evidence to your business, controls and potential consequences.

Understand who, how and why.

Motivation: why your business?

Identify actors reported against your sector and explain their motives, such as payment fraud, extortion, information theft or disruption. Assess why your data, services and relationships might be valuable to them, using dated sources.

Expertise and capability: could they act?

Examine demonstrated skills, tooling and access methods in relevant actor reporting. Map their tradecraft to MITRE ATT&CK where useful, explaining each technique in plain language and identifying what would be needed to use it against your business.

Attack paths: what would they try?

Connect an actor’s documented methods to a specific exposure. For example, an internet-facing login may deserve higher priority when relevant actors use credential attacks and its protections remain unverified. Explain the enabling conditions and which controls would interrupt the path.

Business impact: what needs a decision?

Translate the scenarios into potential operational disruption, fraud, information loss and recovery effort. Where costs are estimated, record the inputs and assumptions so leadership can challenge them.

One assessment. Two useful views.

The engagement includes the agreed written assessment and a briefing for your nominated audience. Audience, depth, scenarios and follow-up sessions are specified in the quote.

Board and leadership briefing

An executive report and briefing covering the most relevant scenarios, business consequences, current control gaps and decisions needed. Clear language supports discussion of investment, ownership and risk acceptance.

Technical threat assessment

Source-backed actor profiles and MITRE ATT&CK mappings connect sector activity to your exposure findings. Your cyber team or IT provider receives the risk rationale, control assumptions and evidence needed to validate each attack path.

Prioritised security roadmap

Prioritised actions explain which part of an attack path they address, what the work involves, who owns it and how to verify the result. The roadmap identifies dependencies and the evidence that would raise or lower each risk judgement.

Detection and response requirements

Identify the activity that would need to be visible to test each scenario: relevant accounts, systems, events and response decisions. These become the starting point for logging, threat hunting and incident response planning.

Fund the controls that address your risk.

A long list of weaknesses does not tell a board what to fund first. A tailored threat assessment helps you compare action against likely business impact, reuse effective controls and explain why a proposed investment deserves priority. It also gives your team a reasoned basis for deferring lower-priority work.

Before you begin

Do we need an exposure report first?

The briefing needs a reliable picture of your exposure. It normally follows our exposure report. If you have recent equivalent findings, we can review their suitability and scope any missing evidence before quoting the briefing.

Is this for directors or technical teams?

Both. We agree the audience before starting. Leadership receives the business implications and decisions; your cyber team or IT provider receives the technical reasoning and action detail. Separate sessions can be included in the agreed scope.

Can you tell us exactly who will attack?

No. Threat intelligence supports a reasoned assessment of intent, capability and opportunity. We distinguish known activity from plausible scenarios and do not present an actor profile as proof that your business is being targeted.

How long does the assessment remain useful?

It reflects the evidence and business context at the time. The report identifies review triggers, such as a new public service, a major supplier change or significant new threat intelligence. Recurring updates are separately scoped.
