01 · Management decision

Executive summary

Example Office’s main website supports normal customer contact. The most important exposure sits outside that current website: a legacy staff portal still answers publicly, but the business cannot identify a current service owner or maintenance arrangement. This warrants urgent ownership and access decisions. It does not establish that the portal has been compromised.

Two other items make a convincing payment or supplier impersonation easier: an outdated finance contact in a downloadable guide, and a project document that reveals internal naming and software details. These can be addressed through normal website maintenance and finance procedures.

Decisions requested this week

Appoint the IT provider to identify and restrict the legacy portal within two business days. Authorise the office manager to replace the supplier guide and remove the unnecessary project document. Retain the general enquiry page: it serves a business purpose and was not raised as a weakness.

Position at issue: one High, two Medium and one Low finding remain open. One positive observation is recorded separately. All recommendations below are proposed actions; none is presented as already completed. The review found exposure, not evidence of an incident.

02 · Coverage

Scope and method

The example authorisation covers the public website, three agreed hostnames, two business profiles and six public documents. The owner’s objective is to understand what an outsider could learn without an account, with particular attention to payment fraud and forgotten services.

Work consists of public-page review, a check of the agreed domain records, document inspection, and a factual validation discussion with the office manager. Evidence is timestamped and mapped to the affected asset. Public availability is separated from inferred business risk: a login page alone does not prove weak passwords or a software vulnerability.

Rating method: High means a significant exposure requiring prompt management attention; Medium means a plausible business risk suitable for planned near-term remediation; Low means limited direct impact or an administrative improvement. Confidence describes the strength of the supporting observation, not the likelihood of an attack. Targets are proposed for this example, not contractual response times.

04 · Evidence and advice

Detailed findings

EXP-01 · High · Open

Legacy staff portal has no confirmed owner

Observation and evidence: EV-01 records a publicly accessible sign-in page at portal.example.test on 7 September at 09:20. EV-02 records the office manager’s statement on 8 September that staff stopped using the portal in 2024 and that no current support contact could be located. The underlying software version and patch state were not established.

Business impact: An unattended service could retain accounts or business data after its intended use has ended. If it is still connected to other systems, the consequences may extend beyond the portal. This potential impact and the ownership gap drive the High priority; there is no claim of demonstrated access.

Recommended action: The IT provider should identify the hosting account, data held, integrations and any remaining users. Restrict access while that review is completed, subject to confirming business dependencies. If redundant, export required business records through an authorised administrator, retire the service and remove obsolete links and domain records.

Effort and dependency: Allow an initial 2–4 hours to identify ownership and dependencies. Migration or decommissioning needs a separate estimate. The business owner must approve any interruption; removing only the website link will not close the exposure.

Closure evidence: Record the owner and decision, then repeat an external access check against the original hostname and any replacement route. Closure requires evidence of retirement or an approved access restriction and maintenance arrangement. A screenshot of a deleted link is insufficient.

EXP-02 · Medium · Open

Supplier guide names a former finance contact

Observation and evidence: EV-03 is an illustrative extract from supplier-guide.pdf, retrieved 7 September at 10:05. It names a former employee and invites suppliers to email payment-detail changes. The office manager confirmed the contact is obsolete in EV-02. No mailbox or payment workflow was tested.

Business impact: An attacker could reuse familiar names and wording in a fraudulent message. The document’s existence does not mean a fraudulent payment would succeed; callback practices and approval controls were outside the public review.

Recommended action: Replace the guide with a current role-based contact and instructions to verify payment changes using a previously trusted contact channel. Update the download link and ask the website provider to remove the superseded public file. The finance lead should check that the published process matches actual practice.

Effort and closure: Approximately 1–2 hours for editing and publication, plus finance approval. Revisit the original file URL and the current download page, retain the approved replacement, and record any external copies that cannot be removed. Publication changes alone do not validate internal payment controls.

EXP-03 · Medium · Open

A project handover document reveals internal details

Observation and evidence: EV-04 records a linked project-handover.pdf on 7 September at 11:10. Its appendix includes internal server names, a remote-support product name and an author’s direct email address. These details have no identified customer-facing purpose. Their continued accuracy is unknown; no passwords were observed in the six reviewed files.

Business impact: Specific technical language can make an impersonated IT support request more credible. Internal names are not credentials and do not themselves provide network access, but they reduce an outsider’s need to guess.

Recommended action: The IT provider should decide whether the file needs to remain public. Replace it with a customer-facing version where needed, remove unnecessary metadata, and review related downloads for the same publication mistake. Do not republish the original as part of the remediation ticket.

Effort and closure: Allow 2–3 hours for the six-file review and replacement preparation. The website editor supplies the approved file and evidence that the original URL no longer serves the sensitive version. Third-party caches and already-downloaded copies remain a residual exposure.

EXP-04 · Low · Open

Public information has no scheduled ownership review

Observation and evidence: In EV-02, the office manager described ad hoc website updates; no review register was supplied. Confidence is Moderate because this is based on the interview and documents provided, not a review of every business procedure.

Business impact: Obsolete contacts and forgotten services can reappear after staff or supplier changes. This is a maintenance weakness, not a separate demonstrated route into the network.

Action and closure: Assign an owner to each domain, profile and download area. Add a quarterly review and a check when key staff leave or a service is retired. Allow 1–2 hours to establish the register. Close after the owner approves the register and records the first completed review, with the next review date.

OBS-01 · Positive observation

Keep the general business enquiry page

EV-05 records a current role-based enquiry address and business telephone number on the contact page. The reviewed page does not publish private residential details. Keep these useful contact routes and include them in the ownership review. Hiding ordinary business contact information would impede customers without resolving the findings above.

05 · Practical next steps

Action plan and dependencies

  1. Within two business days: the business owner appoints the portal owner; the IT provider confirms dependencies and proposes a temporary restriction. Escalate to the business owner if hosting access cannot be recovered.
  2. Within two weeks: replace the two documents, validate their original URLs and have finance approve the new supplier wording. Complete EXP-02 and EXP-03 together to reduce repeated publishing work.
  3. Within 30 days: complete portal retirement or an approved support arrangement, then establish the quarterly public-content register. The portal’s temporary restriction is not a substitute for this decision.

Planning allowance: the initial actions total approximately 6–11 hours of combined IT and business time before any portal migration. These are fictional effort estimates, not a price quotation. Vendor access, data retention decisions and content approval may extend elapsed time.

A client delivery would include an access-controlled evidence pack with original locations, collection times, file integrity records where appropriate, and a redaction record. This public example uses reserved .test names and deliberately omits original files. Evidence references here demonstrate traceability rather than linking to real captures.

The review is a point-in-time sample of agreed public information. Unlinked pages, private content, personal accounts, leaked credentials and archived third-party copies were not exhaustively searched. No exploitation, authenticated testing or compromise investigation was performed. An absence of findings in those areas must not be read as an assurance that they are safe.

07 · Completion

Handover and closure

The proposed walkthrough covers the portal decision first, then document changes and ownership. The business owner confirms priorities; the IT provider and office manager confirm named action owners and achievable dates. Unresolved factual disagreements are added to the finding rather than silently removed.

Each action remains Open until the agreed verification evidence is reviewed. If a risk is accepted instead of fixed, record the approving business owner, reason, compensating measures and expiry date. Remediation work and any subsequent retest must be agreed separately; this example does not imply an ongoing monitoring service.

Delivery checklist: report and findings register; controlled evidence pack; action tracker; walkthrough notes; and a documented list of remaining questions. Version 1.0 is the initial illustrative issue, with no remediation or retest claimed.
