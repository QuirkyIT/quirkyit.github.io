Cyber security training options

Scam-spotting session

One short session for your team on the scams aimed at businesses like yours — fake invoices, supplier bank-change requests and impersonated managers. Everyone leaves with a card showing the one check to make and who to tell.

Staff essentials

A longer session tailored to your own suppliers, systems, payment path and public footprint, so the examples are recognisably yours. Includes a summary for the owner or manager who approves payments, and a note of anything training alone cannot fix.

Whole-business programme

Sessions delivered across sites or shifts so behaviour is consistent everywhere, plus a dedicated module for the people who approve payments and account changes. We run an announced walkthrough, test that your reporting route reaches a real person, and write up the gaps.

Incident response and threat hunting training

Hands-on training for technical staff: holding the first hour of an incident, preserving evidence rather than destroying it, scoping what is affected, and running a bounded hunt. Exercises use realistic evidence shaped to the tools you actually run.

What your training includes

Sessions delivered to your named teams, a one-page verification and reporting card, a checklist for whoever approves payment and access changes, and a record of who attended.

You also receive a short findings note listing the things training could not fix — where a process or a control needs to change instead. That note is often the most useful part.

Cloned voices and AI-written messages have made the convincing version of these scams cheap to produce at scale. Sessions cover what to do when a request sounds exactly like the person making it: verify through a channel your business chose in advance, not the one the message arrived on.

Delivered on site or online, during agreed business hours. Sessions are quoted to suit your team size and how many sites or shifts need covering.

Isn't there free training available?

Yes, and good material too — Cyber Wardens, the ACSC small business guide and Scamwatch are all free and credible. If general awareness is all you need, use those. What we sell is tailoring to your own suppliers and payment process, delivery to real people in a room, a reporting route we test end to end, and a written note of what training cannot fix. If you do not need those, we will say so.

Will this stop our staff being tricked?

No, and nobody can promise that. Convincing scams fool careful people. Training changes how often it works, how fast it gets reported and whether the payment is verified before it leaves — and where the real answer is a process change, the findings note says so.

Do you run fake phishing tests on our staff?

Not by default. Covert simulations are not included, and any walkthrough is announced beforehand. The aim is for people to report quickly without fear of blame, and catching them out works against that.

Is this a certification?

No. There is no certification or accreditation, and it is not legal or insurance advice. We supply a record of who attended, not individual scores.

Our IT provider already covers this

Worth checking what is actually delivered, to whom, how much of it is tailored to your business, and when your reporting route was last tested end to end. Some providers and insurers bundle an awareness platform, and duplicating one is not a service worth paying for.

We did training last year

Recognition fades and the techniques change. A short refresher aimed at what is current works better than repeating the same session.
