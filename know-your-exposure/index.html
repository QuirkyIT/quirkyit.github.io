Exposure & vulnerability assessment

QuirkyIT reviews your public information and checks agreed systems for security weaknesses. We explain what could put your business at risk and give your IT team a prioritised list of fixes.

Email DeanService options

What the assessment covers

Public information review

We review your business websites, public profiles and documents for information that could help someone target you. You receive the findings and practical advice on what to change.

Vulnerability assessment

We scan agreed systems for known security weaknesses, review the results and prioritise the fixes. Your IT provider receives a clear action list with the evidence behind it.

Optional add-on

Personal exposure review

We review publicly visible information about consenting directors or staff and recommend ways to reduce unwanted exposure. Each person receives their findings privately.

What your assessment includes

A report with an executive summary, agreed scope and coverage, detailed findings with evidence and confidence, and a prioritised action plan. Each recommended fix identifies an owner, dependencies and the evidence needed to verify it. We walk through the findings with you and your IT provider.

Because collection and analysis are automated, findings come back quickly rather than after weeks of manual work. Choose a focused initial review or a fuller assessment. System changes and fixes are quoted separately.

This step has become more worth doing early. The reconnaissance an attacker once did by hand is now automated, so information you made public years ago is easier to find and combine today than it was when you published it.

Read a full worked example, including the findings register, action plan and downloadable report template: sample exposure review or sample vulnerability assessment.

Will you need access?

Public exposure reviews need no internal access or software installation. Scanning needs permission and an agreed time.

Is this a penetration test?

No. These assessments identify exposure and known weaknesses without attempting to exploit them.

Does an assessment prove we are secure?

No. An assessment describes what we found, at one point in time, in the systems we agreed to check. It does not prove that nothing exists elsewhere and it is not a guarantee.

Do you fix what you find?

Fixes and system changes are quoted separately. Where you have an IT provider, the report is written so they can action it.
