Where to start

Three steps, each built on the one before it. Start at the top and stop when you have what you need.

Step 1

Exposure & vulnerability assessment →

What can an outsider already see, and what is weak enough to be worth using? Your public information and agreed systems get checked, and your IT team gets a prioritised list of fixes with the evidence behind it.

Step 2

Threat brief →

Built on those findings: which attacks actually apply to your business, what someone would try first, and which fixes are worth doing before the others. Written to be read by whoever decides the budget.

Step 3

Logging, alerts & threat hunting →

Now make it visible. Logging and alerts so an attack would leave a trace, and a hunt through your existing records to check whether one already has.

What AI changes

Attackers now use AI the same way any business does: to work faster and at larger scale. Convincing scam emails and voice calls cost almost nothing to produce. Public information about your business can be gathered and cross-referenced in minutes rather than days. Newly published weaknesses get used sooner after they appear.

None of this is a new kind of attack. It makes the familiar ones cheaper, faster and better aimed, which is why the three steps above matter more than they did two years ago:

  • Knowing what is exposed matters more when the reconnaissance an attacker used to do by hand is now automatic.
  • A threat brief tells you which of these methods realistically apply to your business, instead of leaving you to guess from headlines.
  • Logging and threat hunting matter because AI-assisted attacks blend into normal activity better, so noticing them depends on having the records.
  • Training your team is the direct answer to convincing fake messages, cloned voices and urgent payment requests that look genuine.

No service, ours included, makes a business immune to this. The aim is to remove the easy openings and shorten the time it takes you to notice. Using AI in your business and defending against AI-assisted attacks are different jobs, and we can help with both.
